Open the day club now. Build the rooms as it grows.

Two tracks. Track A opens the Day Club on the house and garden you already have — a modest, mostly-no-construction spend. Track B is the frugal build-up — the 2nd-floor rooms, the nurse's room, remodelling and solar — that turns Aangan into a place that can also keep elders overnight. Every figure is a planning range in ₹ lakh; get 2–3 local quotes before committing.

One design rule first — residents stay on the ground floor

For mobile elders with memory loss, stairs are a fall, wandering and evacuation risk. So care rooms stay downstairs (2 existing bedrooms + adapted drawing room), and the new 2nd floor is for you (the family room) and the live-in nurse + storage. The farmer-quarter add-on room also serves staff. This keeps the people who need protecting at ground level, and the build serves them without endangering them.

You never spend it all at once. Track A opens the club and starts building reputation and a waitlist. Track B follows only when the demand is real. Note the build-up lands a little above the model's bare ₹0.50 Cr Minimum — because you're adding a family room, the nurse's room and solar — but still comfortably under the ₹1.4 Cr Realistic build. That's the honest frugal middle.

On power — this is safety, not comfort

At ~20 hours of grid a day, you have a ~4-hour daily outage. For an ordinary home that's an inconvenience; for elders on an oxygen concentrator or needing reliable night lighting and a water pump, it's a hazard. Size a grid-tied solar system with battery backup to carry the essentials — medical, lights, fans, pump — through the cut, and keep an inverter or small genset as redundancy. Don't treat this as optional; it's part of the care floor.
