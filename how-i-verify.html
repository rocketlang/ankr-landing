How I verify

The terms under which I check other people's published work. They are written down so that anyone whose work I look at knows them beforehand, and so that a reader knows what a result from me does and does not mean.

The short version

  • I work from the written specification and the published files, not from the maker's own tooling.
  • The maker hears the result first, pass or fail.
  • A result is a result, not an endorsement.
  • Every result says what it does not establish.
  • When I am wrong, the correction is dated and left in view.

What I do

1. A second implementation

If a maker's own script agrees with the maker's own release, little has been learned. I write a separate check from the specification and the file formats, and run that. I read the maker's script when it is the only place a rule is written down, and I say so when I have.

2. Count the data, do not relay the summary

Where raw data is public, I download it, check it against its published checksums and count it. A figure on one of these pages marked "counted" was counted here. A figure I am repeating from someone else is attributed to them.

3. Compute it, quote it, or leave it out

Each statement is one of three things: something I computed, something I am quoting with its source, or something I leave unsaid. What I did not read is listed as not read.

4. The check is not adjusted until it passes

A check written against one release is run unchanged against the next. If it stops fitting, that is reported as a refusal. It is not quietly rewritten to agree.

5. I attack my own check before anyone relies on it

The same hour a check is written, I feed it altered files, missing files and garbage. Missing evidence must count as a failure, not as a note. A check that crashes must say that it crashed and not look like a verdict, so each outcome has its own exit code: passed, failed, refused, broke.

What a result means

It means that on a stated date, the files as published agreed with the specification as written, in the respects listed. Nothing more.

  • It is not a statement that the product is secure, safe or fit for any purpose.
  • It is not a statement about files published later, or about a unit in the field.
  • "I could not break it" is a different sentence from "it cannot be broken".
  • Each check prints its own limits on every run, next to the result, so the two travel together.

Who hears first

The person whose work it is hears the result from me privately before anything is public, whether it passed or failed. I do not publish a result its owner has not heard. If the work reached me by a route other than its maker, I say so, and the same rule holds.

What is published afterwards is what was found. A failure is not softened and a pass is not dressed up. Please ask before quoting a result.

When I am wrong

It has happened, and a maker has found faults in my check that I had missed. Those faults are described on the page where the check is published, with the date and with credit to whoever found them. A corrected page keeps a dated line saying what changed. Nothing is silently replaced.

The dated claims on these pages are also rechecked every day against their outside sources: whether an identifier has since entered a list, whether a paper has a newer version, whether published checksums have changed, whether the page served is the page written. A claim that stops holding gets a dated correction.

Independence, and its limits

  • No one has paid for a check, and none is for sale. That is the position as of October 2026.
  • I am not affiliated with any party whose work appears on these pages.
  • ANKR builds software of its own, some of it in neighbouring fields. That is a reason to check my work, not a reason to trust it. The scripts and the steps are published so that you can.
  • I do not rank makers against each other, and I do not publish a critique of a named person.

How the work is done

The checks are commissioned and directed by me. The code is written with an AI assistant. The results are published including the failures of my own checks. I say this plainly because a method that is disclosed is a method, and one that is discovered later is something else.

If you would like your work checked

What makes a check possible:

  1. The specification, in writing, at a fixed version.
  2. The published files, with their checksums.
  3. The values a correct result should produce, published before the check.
  4. If records are chained, at least three linked ones, so the chain itself can be tested and not only one link.

I cannot promise a date, and I may decline. I will tell you when I start.

A seaman's version

A surveyor signs for what he sighted, on the day he sighted it, and writes down what he could not reach. The certificate does not say the ship is sound for ever. It says what was seen, when, and by whom. That is all a check of this kind can honestly be.

This is an analogy and proves nothing about software.

Who is checking

I am a master mariner. I spent 22 years at sea. The 22 years since have been ashore, on the other side of the same industry: ports, container operations and logistics, and then advising on shipping and logistics at TCS, one of the large IT companies.

About 17 of those 44 years have been in technology, old and new, from the systems of the late 1990s to today's AI agents.

At sea, a certificate, a survey and a log entry are how one stranger relies on another. Each says exactly what was sighted, by whom and on what day. I bring that habit to software.

Today I build AI agent systems at ANKR, and I write independent checks of what such systems publish about themselves: signed releases, attestation evidence, evaluation claims and incident data. The worked examples below are that work.
