Inferred from what: a reader's check

The longer form of two comments left under an article on models that have had their refusals edited out. A comment is short and cannot be corrected once posted. This page can, and every change to it is dated.

What this page is

An article published on 15 September 2026, "Capability Was Preserved. Inferred From What?", looks at open models whose refusal behaviour has been removed by editing the weights. Sellers of such models say the refusals went and everything else stayed. The article asks what measurement that second claim rests on.

Two comments were posted under it on 2 October 2026. This page carries what did not fit in them: one of the article's sources read directly, with the lines anyone can check; the ship rules the comments lean on, in the rules' own words; five things that would answer the article's question; and what none of this establishes.

Nothing here is affiliated with the article's author, with the publisher of the model card below, or with anyone else mentioned.

One source, read directly

The article cites the model card for Qwen-3.8-27B-Uncensored. The card is unusually open about its own method, and it is to its credit that what follows can be read off it at all. It says of itself:

"Not independently benchmarked. No MMLU/GSM8K/HumanEval or vision-benchmark comparison against the base model has been run. Capability preservation is inferred from the KL objective alone."
"Re-measure rather than trusting this card. The figures here come from a 100-trial optimization on one prompt set. Your domain, prompts, and scoring will differ."

Three more lines from the same card, read together:

kl_target = 0.01  # below this KL, switch to a refusal-only objective
"Trial scoring used the held-out test splits of the same two datasets, kept strictly separate from the estimation sets."
"The optimizer ran 100 trials and selected trial 38 from the Pareto front."

The KL the card then reports, on the harmless set, is 0.00971.

What follows from that. 0.00971 is just under the configured target of 0.01. The split the card calls held-out is separate from the prompts used to find the refusal direction, and it is the split each of the hundred trials was scored on. So the reported figure shows that the search reached its target on the data it was choosing against. That is evidence the search worked. It is not an independent measurement, because a number that was steered to cannot also be the witness for where it ended up.

What does not follow. That this model lost capability. Nothing was run here, and the card may well be right that the edit is narrow. The point is only about what that one figure can carry.

The ship rules, in their own words

The comments compare an edited model to an altered ship. The rules are quoted here so the comparison can be checked and not taken on trust.

After an alteration: when calculation stops being enough

"Where any alterations are made to a ship so as to materially affect the stability information supplied to the master, amended stability information shall be provided. If necessary the ship shall be re-inclined. The ship shall be re-inclined if anticipated deviations exceed one of the values specified in paragraph 5." SOLAS chapter II-1, regulation 5, paragraph 4

Paragraph 5 gives the values: a deviation from the lightship displacement exceeding 2%, or a deviation of the longitudinal centre of gravity exceeding 1% of L.

So re-inclining is not automatic. Amended information is always owed. A small change is handled by calculation. Past a stated deviation, a measurement is required. The rule names the point where calculation stops being enough.

Lifting gear: tested above its working load, and again after repair

"Every lifting appliance and every item of loose gear shall be tested in accordance with national laws or regulations by a competent person before being put into use for the first time and after any substantial alteration or repair to any part liable to affect its safety." ILO Convention 152, article 22, paragraph 1
"Load test means a test in excess of the SWL, carried out in the presence of a competent person in order to check the structural integrity of the lifting appliance and its attachment to and adequacy of its supporting structure." IMO MSC.1/Circ.1663, guidelines for lifting appliances

The test load is heavier than anything the gear will be asked to lift. A test made with a lighter weight would pass the gear and establish less than its certificate says.

The stability booklet: what it is expected to carry

The 2008 Intact Stability Code, part B, section 3.6.7, lists among the contents to consider: "standard operating conditions and examples for developing other acceptable loading conditions using the information contained in the stability booklet". Part B is recommendation, not mandate.

These texts were read on a rules mirror (regulation 5, article 22, Circ.1663), not on the sites of the IMO or the ILO. Check them against a consolidated edition before relying on the wording.

What would answer the question

Five things, the first four owed by whoever ships the edited weights and the fifth by whoever uses them.

  1. Both models, side by side. The same questions, the same harness, the same precision and settings, and both scores published. The card above already asks for this: "Always report the base model alongside it."
  2. Tested on data the edit was not chosen against. A split that was used to pick the winning trial is not available as evidence for it.
  3. Tested where care is the right answer. Law, compliance, and questions whose honest answer is "I do not know", reported by subject and with the number of questions under each figure. An average can stay nearly still while a few subjects move a great deal.
  4. A list of what was not tested. A sheet that says "not tested: multi-turn, non-English, tool use" is worth more than one that says nothing was lost.
  5. The user calibrates before signing. A firm that tests a client's defences with an edited model should score that model against its base, on its own kind of work, before it puts the client's name on a report. If the instrument is weaker than assumed, a clean result overstates the defence.

Added 2 October: who runs the side-by-side

The article's author replied to the comments with a dependency in step 1, and he is right. If the seller runs both columns, the side-by-side is still the seller's own calculation. It becomes a test only when someone else can rerun it on the same harness.

The ship rule sits between those two. The yard conducts the inclining test itself, and what makes it more than the yard's calculation is who is standing there:

"An Administration representative should be present to witness the inclining test and the test results be submitted for review. The responsibility for making preparations, conducting the inclining test and lightweight survey, recording the data, and calculating the results rests with the shipyard, owner or naval architect." 2008 Intact Stability Code, part B, 8.2.1 (recommendation, read on a rules mirror)

So there are three grades, not two:

  1. The seller runs both columns. That is the yard's calculation.
  2. The seller runs them with a witness present and the raw results handed over for review. That is the inclining test as ships do it.
  3. Someone else reruns it on the same harness. That is more than the ship rule asks.

Step 1 as first written on this page asked only for the first grade. Read it as asking for at least the second.

What ships have and edited models do not is the second sentence of the regulation: a stated deviation past which calculation is no longer accepted and a measurement is required. This page does not know what that threshold should be for a model. It only notes that none is written down.

What this page does not establish

  • Nothing was run. No model was loaded and no benchmark was repeated. Everything above is a reading of published text.
  • One model card was read, not all of them. The article cites three cards from the same publisher. Only the 27B card was read for this page.
  • The benchmark run mentioned in the second comment is one run. It was made by one practitioner, who describes it as an observation and not a conclusion and lists its own confounds. It is better read in his own words than through anyone's summary, including the comment's.
  • The research papers the article cites were not read for this page.
  • The ship is an analogy. A ship's lightship weight is one number with an agreed method for measuring it. A model's capability is neither. The comparison shows what a rule with a threshold looks like, and proves nothing about models.
